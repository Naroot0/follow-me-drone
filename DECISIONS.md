| 32 | 2026-11-10 | Behaviour when the person turns around. (a) If the person turns and closes by less than 1 m, the drone hovers and waits. | 33 | 2026-11-10 | Distance control is proportional plus a feedforward of the person's estimated speed: forward speed = person's speed away from the drone + gain x range error. The person's speed is re-estimated every control cycle (10 Hz) from the filtered rate of change of range plus the drone's own forward speed, so it follows a changing pace. The drone may fly backward only to recover the distance lost while braking after the person stops: at up to 0.5 m/s, for at most 1.0 m per recovery, starting within 2 s of its own forward flight, and never while the person is approaching. A drone that has been hovering does not back away; if the person walks toward it, it hovers and yaws, and stays put after the person stops (decision 32). The rear rangefinder stops any reverse at the 1.5 m obstacle distance. | Proportional control alone; a higher proportional gain; a wider distance band while moving; no reverse at all | A proportional term alone must trail by speed divided by gain: 3.9 m at a gain of 1.0 and 4.8 m at the original 0.6 for a person walking 1.4 m/s, outside the 2.0 to 3.0 m band. A gain high enough to close that gap amplifies range noise. Without reverse the drone ends 1.0 to 1.5 m from a person who stops suddenly and stays there. Offline simulation of a three-minute variable-pace walk: 92 % of the time in band with exact range, 86 % with 10 % range noise, against 28 % for proportional control alone. The feedforward depends on the range estimate, the noisiest signal in the system (risk 4); SITL and flight data decide the gains. |
